## 1. The Decision and Why

**We're going to Mutianyu.**

The call comes down to the one thing we can't change: our date is a **Saturday**, and the two sections fail differently on Saturdays.

1. **Crowds are Badaling's structural weak point on a weekend.** Badaling is the most-visited section of the wall, and it operates under a **daily visitor cap of 65,000 (75% of capacity)** that reliably binds on weekends. Guides are blunt about it: "Your date is decided by availability, not the other way round. If the trip has one possible day, book the moment the window opens." We have exactly one possible day. Mutianyu, by contrast, is consistently described by recent visitors as "truly breathtaking and far less crowded than Badaling" (Tripadvisor, Sep 2026), and it has no hard cap that turns our fixed date into a lottery.

2. **The lifts are running at Mutianyu, and our date clears the maintenance calendar.** The enclosed cable car (to Tower 14) and the chairlift/toboggan combo (to Tower 6) are both operational. Mutianyu's enclosed cable car takes an annual maintenance window in late winter/early spring — most recently **March 10–14, 2026** — which ended March 13. **March 6 sits ahead of that window**, but we will confirm the 2027 schedule on the official notices page ~30 days out. Badaling's North Line aerial cableway, by contrast, has a scheduled suspension running **October 21 – December 25, 2026**, with the south ground cableway as the only fallback — a documented pattern of long lift outages.

3. **The weather outlook is meaningfully better at Mutianyu.** Early March in Yanqing is 3–5 °C colder than Huairou at the wall, and the long-range outlook for our date flags **light freezing rain** and stronger wind at Badaling versus merely **patchy rain possible** at Mutianyu. Badaling's North Section ridges — including the 40–50-degree gradients around Hero Slope at Tower 8 — are fully exposed to that wind. Freezing rain plus an exposed steep ridge is the single biggest thing that could wreck the day.

4. **March is off-season hours at Badaling, which kills the late-afternoon light.** From November 1 to March 31, Badaling's wall closes at **16:00**, nearly two hours before the 18:12 sunset. Mutianyu runs weekend hours to **18:30** (ticket check ends 18:00), so we can be on the wall through the golden hour right up to the 18:09 sunset. If we want the evening light, Badaling in March simply cannot offer it.

5. **The 17-minute drive difference doesn't buy anything.** Badaling is 74.4 km / ~1 h 15 min versus Mutianyu at 77.3 km / ~1 h 32 min. We give up ~17 minutes of driving and gain better ratings, lighter crowds, later closing, and warmer conditions.

**Where Badaling would win instead:** if we wanted the 20-minute high-speed train from Qinghe, the most-photographed classic postcard stretch, or the night illumination (that runs late April–early October, not in March). None of those apply to a Saturday in early March with a car.

## 4. Visitor Notes

### Mutianyu — what recent visitors actually say

**Crowding**
- Consistently reported as "far less crowded than Badaling" (Tripadvisor, Sep 2026). The Towers 6→1 stretch is repeatedly described as near-empty: "instead of crowds, we found ourselves alone at several points."
- The busy part is Towers 14→20 — visitors report "literally waiting in line to climb narrow stairs" at pinch points, though "despite the occasional congestion, the atmosphere was calm."
- Arrive early: "crowds start pouring in around 10 or 11 a.m., even in the off-season."
- Avoid Mondays at Mutianyu specifically — Beijing's museums close then, funneling city tourists to the wall. Not an issue for our Saturday.

**Ticketing**
- Real-name tickets, bookable **1–30 days ahead** via the official site (en.mutianyugreatwall.com) or the WeChat account 慕田峪长城. Foreign visitors book with a passport and **scan the original passport directly at the gate** — no ticket exchange needed.
- Visa/Mastercard, Alipay, and WeChat Pay are all accepted.
- Prices: admission ¥40 (postcard ticket ¥45), round-trip shuttle ¥15 (recommended — ~3 km from gate to the wall foot), enclosed cable car ¥140 round trip. Package of admission + shuttle + cable car ≈ ¥180–200. Under 1.2 m free.

**Cable car and toboggan — operating status**
- **Enclosed cable car** (to Tower 14): operational. It undergoes periodic annual maintenance windows — Dec 2–4 2025, Dec 13 2025, and **Mar 10–14 2026** (service resumed Mar 13, 2026). Our date clears the historical early-March window; **verify the 2027 notice ~30 days out**.
- **Chairlift + toboggan** (to/from Tower 6): operational. Runs 08:30–17:30 (admission ends 17:00).
- **Critical ticketing trap:** the cable car and the chairlift/toboggan are run by **two separate companies — tickets are NOT interchangeable.** If you want cable car up + toboggan down, buy **two one-way tickets (¥100 + ¥100)**, not the ¥140 round trip. This is the single most common mistake foreign visitors make at Mutianyu.
- **Toboggan rules:** closed when it rains; not permitted for visitors over 60; queues can hit 1–2 hours at peak. One rider reported an hour's wait at 14:30 and called it "the highlight of the day."

**Hours (current listing)**
- Mon–Fri 07:30–18:00 · Sat–Sun 07:30–18:30. Weekend ticket sales end 17:30, ticket check ends 18:00.

**Other things that could wreck the day**
- Weather: the site closes temporarily for storms/visibility — there were same-day closure notices in the 2026 summer monsoon season. Watch the official notices page.
- Physical demand: "need to be fit and limber... steep small stairs and a few tight spaces" (Sep 2026). Towers 1–6 are the steepest stretch; Tower 20 is the high point.
- Bring water and snacks; on-wall prices are inflated (bottled water ¥5). No shelter on the wall — sunscreen and a packable rain layer.
- Passports are checked at the gate; don't leave them at the hotel.

**Ratings**
- Google: ticket office **4.8 / 5 (1,600 ratings)**; the wall POI itself **4.4 / 5 (19,298 ratings)**.
- Tripadvisor: **4.8 / 5 (24,563 reviews)**, Travelers' Choice "Best of the Best" 2026.

### Badaling — what recent visitors actually say

**Crowding**
- The most-visited section of the wall. On weekends and Chinese holidays it is "very busy," and the **65,000 daily cap (75% of capacity) reliably sells out**. Booking guides warn that on a fixed date, "your date is decided by availability."
- Crowd escape valve if we ever go: most visitors stop at Tower 8 (Hero Slope) for photos and turn back — "if you continue walking north past Tower 8 toward Tower 12, the crowds drop off dramatically within 15 minutes." The South Section runs at roughly 30% of the North Section's volume.
- Timing tricks that work: arrive by 08:00 before the tour buses, or arrive 13:30–14:00 as groups leave for dinner.

**Ticketing**
- Real-name, **online-only** (badalinggreatwall.com or the WeChat mini-program), sales open **10 days ahead**, one ticket per passport per day. No walk-up purchase at the gate. Note: the Beijing municipal English listing states no reservation is required and on-site purchase is possible — reports conflict, so plan on booking online.
- Prices: admission ¥35 off-season (Nov–Mar) / ¥40 peak; cable car ¥100 one-way, ¥140 return.

**Cable car — operating status**
- North Line aerial cableway (to Tower 7/8): **suspended October 21 – December 25, 2026** for an unexplained closure. The **South Line ground cableway** (to South Tower 4) remained running as the fallback.
- Lesson for March 2027: confirm both lines' status before we commit; extended suspensions are an established pattern here.

**Hours (current listing)**
- Peak season (Apr 1–Oct 31): 06:30–16:30. **Off-season (Nov 1–Mar 31): 07:30–16:00.** Our date falls in off-season hours — no evening or sunset access.
- Night illumination tours run only late April–early October; not available in March.

**Other things that could wreck the day**
- The Hero Slope between North Towers 7 and 8 is steep (40–50° gradients) and exposed to strong wind; visitors call it "a literal calf-killer." In freezing rain it would be genuinely hazardous.
- One Oct 2026 review describes a stranger attempting to lure a solo female traveler away from the funicular exit on the wall — worth being aware of, especially for anyone wandering alone.
- Bus 877 departs near Beitucheng station (routing recently changed from Deshengmen); the high-speed train from Qinghe is ~20 minutes. Neither applies to us as a driving group.
- Drones are prohibited.
